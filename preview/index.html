Getting started

How we’ll work together

Taking the first step is the hardest part of therapy, but I’m here to make everything easier.

First, we’ll have a no-obligation chat, which is a chance for me to learn about what’s important to you, and for you to learn how I can help you move forward.

Each session is tailored to your needs as an individual, because therapy isn’t just about approaches and exercises. It’s a space we create, where you feel able to share, learn, and reclaim your power.

  1. Our first conversation

    Our first conversation is an opportunity to get to know each other. I’ll ask a little about what’s brought you to counselling, what you’re hoping to achieve and how you’ve been feeling.

    You can ask questions too. It’s important that you feel comfortable with the person you’re talking to. There’s no obligation to proceed with therapy.

  2. Your online counselling sessions

    We’ll meet online, so you can attend sessions from somewhere private and comfortable without having to travel.

    Sessions with me can include exploring difficult experiences, understanding your feelings, and developing practical ways to manage everyday changes that work for you.

    Everything is on your terms. I don’t expect you to share anything before you’re ready.

  3. Moving forward at your own pace

    There’s no fixed timeline for feeling better. Some people come to counselling with a specific concern, while others need more time to understand what they’re experiencing.

    We’ll regularly check in on how you’re feeling and whether our sessions are helping you move towards your goals.

Frequently asked questions

Can’t find what you’re looking for? See more FAQs or get in touch.

Where do you offer online counselling services?

I provide confidential online counselling for adults across Ireland and the UK. Whether you’re based in Dublin, Cork, Limerick, Galway, Tipperary or elsewhere, you can access support without travelling to an appointment.

All you need is a reliable internet connection and a private, comfortable space where you feel able to talk freely. I’ll confirm that online counselling is suitable and that I can provide services in your location before we begin.

In-person vs online therapy: which is better for me?

Both online and in-person counselling can be effective. The right choice depends on your preferences, circumstances and the type of support you need.

Online therapy offers flexibility, privacy and convenience. You can talk to a therapist online from your own home, which may feel more comfortable if you’re anxious about attending appointments or have a busy schedule.

However, some people prefer being in the same room as their therapist, and certain difficulties may require in-person or specialist support.

We can discuss whether online counselling is a suitable option for you during our initial conversation.

How many counselling sessions will I need?

There’s no set number of counselling sessions that works for everyone.

If you’re seeking support with a specific issue, such as managing anxiety or recognising unhelpful thought patterns, a shorter course of CBT-informed counselling may be appropriate.

Other concerns, particularly those involving difficult life experiences or long-standing patterns, may benefit from longer-term support.

We’ll discuss your goals and review your progress together, so you can make informed decisions about continuing therapy.

Who is CBT not suitable for?

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) can be effective for many difficulties, including anxiety and depression, but it isn’t necessarily the right approach for everyone.

CBT often involves exploring thoughts and behaviours and practising techniques between sessions. Some people prefer a less structured, more exploratory approach, while others may need specialist or more intensive support.

Certain complex mental health difficulties may require CBT to be adapted or combined with other treatments.

As a counsellor and psychotherapist, I recognise that everyone’s needs are different. I’ll discuss your circumstances with you and, if another type of support would be more appropriate, help you consider your options.

Can I talk to a therapist online about more than one issue?

Absolutely. It’s common for anxiety, low confidence, relationship difficulties and other emotional challenges to overlap.

You don’t need to choose just one issue to discuss or have a clear explanation for how you’re feeling.

We’ll explore what’s happening in your life and focus on the areas that matter most to you.

Is online counselling confidential?

Yes. Confidentiality is an important part of counselling, and our sessions provide a private space for you to discuss personal concerns.

Before we begin, I’ll explain how your information is handled, the steps we take to protect your privacy and the limited circumstances in which confidentiality may need to be broken, such as serious safety concerns.

I also recommend attending your online sessions somewhere private where you won’t be overheard.
