The good news

Anxiety is highly treatable, and CBT can help you regain a sense of calm and control.

You’re not alone

Do you feel anxious most of the time?

You may be experiencing

  • Constant worry
  • Racing thoughts
  • Overthinking
  • Difficulty relaxing
  • Feeling on edge

Anxiety can affect your confidence, relationships and everyday life.

Anxiety can affect your entire life, sneaking into your daily routines to impact your mental, physical and emotional wellbeing. It frequently causes difficulty concentrating, persistent worry about worst-case scenarios, and avoidance behaviours that can cause you to miss out on new experiences.

CBT aims to stop negative cycles such as these by breaking down things that make you feel bad, anxious or scared. By making your problems more manageable, CBT can help you change your negative thought patterns and improve the way you feel.

CBT can help you get to a point where you can achieve this on your own and tackle problems without the help of a therapist.

Understanding anxiety

The CBT cycle

Anxiety can create a cycle, but change is possible.

1 Thoughts

Worrying, negative thinking, “what if?” thoughts.

2 Feelings

Anxiety, fear, overwhelm, unease.

3 Behaviours

Avoiding situations, seeking reassurance, withdrawing.

4 More anxiety

The cycle continues and anxiety grows.

CBT helps you break the cycle and take back control.

CBT can help you

  • Understand your thoughts
  • Reduce worry and fear
  • Change unhelpful patterns
  • Build confidence and coping skills
  • Feel more calm, clear and in control

You don’t have to face anxiety on your own.

Support is here, and things can get better.

Grief and loss

Counselling for bereavement, grief & loss

There are many types of loss: loss of a job, the end of a relationship, the loss of health, the death of a loved one, a pet.

The death of a loved one can be the biggest loss in our lives.

Bereavement and loss affect people in different ways, but most people experience grief in some way. Grief includes the deep sadness after a death or loss. It is a natural process as we learn how to live without the person who has died. Sometimes we need help.
