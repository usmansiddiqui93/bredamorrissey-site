Supporting Dementia Caregivers: A Guide to CBT and ACT Therapies

Breda MorrisseyAccredited counsellor & CBT psychotherapist

Caring for a loved one with dementia is one of the most profound acts of love and dedication a person can undertake. Yet, it is also a journey that demands an immense physical, emotional, and psychological toll. When you are entirely focused on managing someone else’s changing reality, memory loss, and daily safety, it is incredibly easy for your own well-being to slip into the background.

At Breda Morrissey CBT and Counselling, I frequently meet caregivers who are operating on absolute empty. If you are caring for a partner, parent, or relative with dementia, please know this: feeling overwhelmed, exhausted, or even guilty does not mean you are failing. It means you are human, and you deserve support too.

The Invisible Weight of Caregiving

Dementia doesn't just impact the person diagnosed; it fundamentally changes the lives of their family members. Caregivers often experience what is known as "anticipatory grief"—grieving the loss of the person as they once were, even while they are still here.

Over time, the chronic stress of caregiving can manifest as:

  • Persistent Anxiety and Overthinking: Constantly worrying about what might happen next or whether you are doing enough.
  • The "Guilt Trap": Feeling guilty for wanting a break, losing your patience, or feeling resentful about the situation.
  • Physical Exhaustion and Isolation: Withdrawing from friends, hobbies, and social networks due to a lack of time or emotional energy.

If left unaddressed, this level of sustained stress can lead to burnout. Fortunately, evidence-based psychological therapies like Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) and Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) offer powerful tools to help you navigate these complex emotions.

How Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Can Help

CBT is a practical, goal-oriented approach that looks at the relationship between our thoughts, feelings, and behaviours. For a dementia caregiver, CBT can be transformative in breaking unhelpful thinking loops:

  • Challenging "All-or-Nothing" Thoughts: Caregivers often hold themselves to impossible standards (e.g., "If I ask for respite care, I’m abandoning them"). CBT helps you identify these harsh assumptions and replace them with more balanced, realistic perspectives ("Taking a break allows me to be a healthier, more patient caregiver").
  • Managing Behavioral Triggers: Dementia can sometimes cause behavioral changes or confusion. CBT helps you step back, process your automatic emotional reactions, and find calmer, more problem-focused ways to respond.

How Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) Can Help

While CBT helps you manage your thinking, ACT focuses on changing your relationship with difficult emotions and situations. It is particularly effective for dementia care because it acknowledges a difficult truth: you cannot change the diagnosis, but you can change how you live alongside it.

  • Practicing Radical Acceptance: Acceptance doesn’t mean giving up. In ACT, it means stopping the exhausting internal battle against reality. Accepting that a situation is incredibly difficult—and that it’s okay to feel sad or angry about it—actually frees up emotional energy.
  • Reconnecting with Your Values: Caregiving can completely consume your identity. ACT helps you reconnect with what matters to you as an individual (e.g., connection, creativity, or self-care) and helps you find small, manageable ways to weave those values back into your week.
  • Mindfulness in the Present: ACT teaches you to drop an anchor in the present moment when the future feels too overwhelming to contemplate.

You Cannot Pour From an Empty Cup

Seeking therapy as a caregiver isn’t a sign that you can’t handle things; it is a vital strategy to ensure you can survive the journey intact. You deserve a dedicated, non-judgmental space to vent, process your grief, and learn how to hold boundaries for your own mental health.
